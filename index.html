Our mission

Get every kid in Europe playing by turning empty courts, school halls and community spaces into busy youth-sport hubs.

We start with one warehouse academy and use it to train coaches. Those coaches then run programmes in the schools, community centres and halls already in each neighbourhood, with our software, investment and programmes, on a profit-sharing basis. Then we repeat the model city by city across Europe.

At a glance

The strategy: hub first, then partner venues

1 · Why a warehouse hub first?

A place of our own gives us a court to train coaches on, a showcase to show schools and councils, a home for the elite squads, and a multi-use timetable that earns money all day. Coaches come to us because we have a court. Once the hub is running, recruiting and training coaches gets much easier.

2 · Why partner venues next?

Europe is full of school halls, community centres and courts that sit empty most of the week. We bring coaches, programmes and software, and the venue brings the space. Each new site costs ~€4k instead of ~€360k, and it sits right next to the kids. See Partner venues.

3 · Why basketball?

One court and one coach can run 20–40 kids, far more per coach than any racket sport. The same floor also converts to badminton, pickleball, yoga, dance and seniors' sessions. See Why basketball and Timetable.

4 · Why Europe?

Basketball is growing fast, indoor space is scarce, and the EU funds youth sport (Erasmus+ Sport up to €400k per project). See Best cities, Start a business and EU funding.

Roadmap

🧠 Expert review: what to validate before investing click to expand

1. Test demand before signing a lease

Run 8–12 weeks of paid sessions in hired school halls first. Measure sign-ups, attendance and churn. This turns the calculator's occupancy guess into real data.

2. Find the building first

Rent per m² and a clear height of 7 m+ decide whether the hub works at all. Get 3 real warehouse quotes and put them into the Calculator.

3. Lock in coaches early

Coaches are the product. Check local qualification rules (France needs a state diploma plus a professional card; Ireland needs Garda vetting) before choosing a country.

4. Sign one partner school early

A letter of intent from one school or council proves the partner-venue model to investors and grant bodies.

5. Set up the non-profit arm

Most grants require a non-profit club. Register it alongside the company so you're ready for the 2027 Erasmus+ round.

6. Biggest risks

Low occupancy in year 1; rent; coach turnover; one partner venue cancelling; safeguarding incidents. Each has a mitigation: pilot first, profit-share deals, a coach academy, a diversified venue base, strict vetting.

Partner venues: the asset-light network

We turn underused spaces such as vacant or after-hours schools, community centres, colleges, public courts, pools and table-tennis halls into active youth-sport venues. We bring coaches, programmes, software, equipment and marketing. The venue brings the space. We share the profit. It starts once the hub is running and training coaches.

The honest picture. Basketball wins clearly on kids per coach and coach cost per kid, and staff costs come every month while a fit-out is paid once. Squash fits a few more kids into the same space but needs one coach per court. Badminton and pickleball courts are cheaper to fit out, but every court still needs its own coach. The basketball floor can also host badminton and pickleball off-peak (see Timetable), so it gets their income too.

Other reasons basketball fits a kids' academy

👥 Team sport = kids stay

Kids come back for their friends and their team.

🏫 Schools already play it

It's part of PE across Europe, which makes recruiting and partnerships easier.

🎒 Cheap for families

Just trainers and a ball. No racket and no court booking.

🔁 One floor, many uses

The same floor hosts camps, 3×3, badminton, pickleball, yoga, dance and futsal.

📈 Growing in Europe

3×3 is an Olympic sport, Europe has NBA stars, and a European NBA league is planned.

⚖️ Boys and girls together

Plenty of grants are aimed at getting girls into sport.

        📍 Choosing the exact neighbourhood site checklist

        🏫 Near schools

        3+ schools within 1 km (a 10–15 min walk). Kids come straight from school, and the schools become your partner venues.

        🏘️ In residential areas

        Dense family housing; check census data for households with children.

        🚌 Easy to reach

        Public transport within 5 minutes, safe cycling routes, and a drop-off area.

        📐 The building

        7 m+ clear height, ~1,000 m² of floor. Light-industrial units are usually cheapest.

        ⚔️ Competition

        Map clubs within 3 km. Waiting lists mean demand isn't being met.

        💶 Ability to pay

        Mid-level incomes, plus grant-funded free places in low-income areas.
