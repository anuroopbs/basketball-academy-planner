🏀 Basketball Academy Planner

Youth basketball academies across Europe

A network of basketball academies for kids aged 8–18, set up in dense family neighbourhoods close to schools, so getting to training is a short walk or bus ride after the school day. Adult sessions, pickup games and camps fill the court hours that kids don't use.

At a glance

The plan

🎯 Who it's for

Kids and teens aged 8–18, grouped by age and level (8–11 fundamentals, 12–14 development, 15–18 competitive). Adults use the courts in the evenings and on weekends.

📍 Where it goes

Dense residential areas within walking distance of several schools. We rent sports halls by the hour at first and only build our own courts once demand is proven. See Best cities.

💶 How it makes money

Weekly kids coaching is the core. Camps, adult training, pickup games, court rental, parties and sponsorship add to it. See Revenue streams.

🇪🇺 How it's funded

Founder capital plus EU (Erasmus+ Sport) and national sport grants, applied for through a non-profit club partner. See EU funding.

EU funding

Grants and finance available to a youth basketball academy in the EU, with amounts. Checked 14 October 2026.

Read this first. Most EU and national sport grants go to non-profit organisations and pay for projects (a programme, an event, a coach exchange), not day-to-day running costs. The usual setup is a company that runs the academy plus a registered non-profit club or association that applies for grants. Erasmus+ also needs partner organisations in other countries, which suits a multi-city academy well.
Timing. The 2026 Erasmus+ Sport deadline was 5 March 2026, so it has passed. The next round is normally announced in the autumn with a deadline in early spring. Plan to apply for the 2027 round and watch the EU Funding & Tenders portal.

Scoring factors and weights

Each city gets a score from 1 to 10 on every factor. The final score is a weighted average out of 100. The default weights favour what matters most for a kids' academy: lots of families and schools close together.

Choosing the exact location inside a city

A city-level score only says where to start. The real decision is the neighbourhood, and the school and housing factors only really count at that level. Look for a site that meets these:

🏫 Near schools

At least 3 primary or secondary schools within 1 km (a 10–15 minute walk). Kids can come straight from school and parents don't have to drive. Schools are also where you recruit and run partnerships.

🏘️ In residential areas

Dense family housing (apartments and terraces), not business districts. Check census data for the share of households with children aged 5–17.

🚌 Easy to reach

Bus, tram or metro stop within 5 minutes, safe walking and cycling routes, and a short-stay drop-off spot for parents.

🕓 Hall free when kids are

Indoor hall available weekdays 15:30–20:00 and weekend mornings. School and municipal halls are often free at exactly these times and rent by the hour.

⚔️ Competition

Map existing basketball clubs within 3 km. Long waiting lists at nearby clubs are a good sign of unmet demand.

💶 Ability to pay

Medium household income keeps the fee affordable. Mix in grant-funded free places for low-income areas (these also strengthen grant applications).
